# Cadence Templates

Kyle Sanders · Schneider Electric · Western US Business Development · EAE

Post-meeting follow-up sequences by account archetype. Pick the template that matches the account, copy the checklist into the call log, and put every dated item in the CRM the same day as the meeting.

**Governing principle:** cadence is driven by *triggers*, not by the calendar. A follow-up with no new information is a withdrawal from the relationship account, not a deposit. When there is nothing new to say, wait for the trigger instead of inventing a touch.

---

## Template 1 — Integrator / Channel Recruit

**Use when:** the counterparty is a systems integrator, OEM, or machine builder who could deliver EAE to *their* customers. Their own facility's use case is irrelevant.

**The real objective:** get EAE installed on their machine and get them through free virtual training. Everything else is theater until that happens.

| When | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| T+24h | Recap email | Answer every open technical question or name the date you'll have it. Confirm the next session. Name the engineer and why they're credible (e.g. ex-Rockwell). State the agenda back **in the integrator's own words**. Attach nothing else — no brochure. |
| T+24h | Send trial license + training enrollment link | Do not wait for the technical session. The license is the ask, not the reward. |
| Next session | Engineer working session, 45 min | Not a feature demo. Build something small, then swap the controller underneath and show what the changeover actually involves. Hands on keyboard, theirs if possible. |
| T+7d after session | Short note | One concrete thing they could build with it in their own book of work. No "just checking in." |
| **T+30d** | **The qualifier: "Did you get a chance to spin it up?"** | This is the single highest-signal question in the sequence. Installed = real, invest further. Not installed after 30 days = not real yet, drop to quarterly. |
| T+30d if installed | Introduce to partner/channel program | Certification path, project registration, deal support, co-selling. |
| Quarterly if not | Low-cost touch tied to a release | Safety roadmap milestone, new library, local training date, user group. |
| Trigger | They win a job that fits | Re-enter immediately with engineering support. This is the conversion moment. |

**Do not** push the integrator's own plant as an opportunity. It muddies the relationship and makes you look like you're selling past the point.

## Template 2 — End User, Trigger Identified

**Use when:** gate ≥ 3.0 with a funded project and a date.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| T+24h | Recap email: pain in their words, Phase 1 scope, **the ~$4–6k number in writing**, proposed next step with two date options |
| T+3d | Technical deep-dive with engineer — Phase 1 overlay architecture against their actual stack |
| T+10d | Phase 1 proposal: scope, cost, timeline, what stays untouched (I/O, wiring, cabinets, existing PLC logic) |
| T+14d | Multi-threading call — bring in whoever owns the budget or the standard |
| Project milestone | Align every touch to their engineering schedule, not yours |
| Decision date | Ask for it explicitly and put it in the CRM |

## Template 3 — End User, No Trigger (Nurture)

**Use when:** gate ≤ 2.0. Relationship is fine; timing is not.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| T+24h | Short recap. Thank them for the transparency. No pressure, no next meeting invented. |
| — | **Identify and record the re-entry trigger**: next rebuild, obsolescence date, capital cycle, retirement of the incumbent engineer. Put the date in the CRM. |
| Quarterly | One genuinely useful item — reference story from their segment, roadmap milestone that closes a gap they named, local training date |
| At trigger | Re-enter with a specific reason tied to what they told you |

Three quarterly touches with no engagement = archive. Being able to archive is what keeps the rest of the pipeline honest.

## Template 4 — Technical Blocker Identified

**Use when:** a real product gap (today: functional safety, synchronized motion) is the stated reason for no.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| In-meeting | Concede honestly and immediately. Then **isolate**: "If safety weren't a factor, is there an application here?" The answer tells you whether the blocker is the reason or the excuse. |
| T+48h | Written answer from the product team on roadmap timing. Precise. No "soon." |
| T+7d | If isolated fit exists, propose a Phase 1 overlay scope that lives entirely outside the blocked function |
| Roadmap date | Calendar the milestone. Re-enter the week it ships — this is the strongest cold-restart reason you will ever have. |

**Known gaps as of this writing:** functional safety (roadmap ~late 2027), synchronized/coordinated motion. Say both plainly. Credibility on the gaps is what makes the rest believable.

## Partner briefing checklist

Run before any call where a channel partner or internal resource attends. Fifteen minutes, no exceptions. An unbriefed partner in the room is a liability.

- [ ] Account background and gate score shared
- [ ] Which of the three positioning angles you're leading with — and which two you are *not*
- [ ] **Known product gaps named, with agreed language for each**
- [ ] Explicit: no roadmap speculation in front of the prospect
- [ ] Who answers what — and who says "let me get you the right person"
- [ ] Desired next step, so you both close toward the same thing

---

## Pre-meeting checklist

- [ ] Gate scored, meeting type authorized
- [ ] Trigger event identified and stated back in the opening
- [ ] Demo/IDE environment **open and working** before the call starts
- [ ] Backup for the room's AV failing (own hotspot, own screen, printed one-pager)
- [ ] Two specific date options ready for the next step
- [ ] Phase 1 pricing at hand and rehearsed out loud
- [ ] Partner briefed (if attending)
