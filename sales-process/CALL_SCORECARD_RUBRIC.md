# EAE Call Scorecard Rubric

Kyle Sanders · Schneider Electric · Western US Business Development
Scoring standard for first meetings, discovery calls, and technical follow-ups on EcoStruxure Automation Expert.

**Purpose:** score every recorded conversation the same way, so improvement is measurable instead of felt. The rubric is deliberately harder on *pre-call qualification* than on *in-call polish*, because target selection drives outcomes more than technique does.

---

## How to use

1. Score **Part A (Pre-Call Gate)** *before* the meeting. It decides whether the meeting happens and what kind of meeting it is.
2. Score **Part B (In-Call Execution)** from the transcript, within 24 hours.
3. Score **Part C (Outcome)** once the next step is either booked or confirmed dead.
4. File the result in `sales-process/calls/YYYY-MM-DD-account-slug.md` using the template at the bottom.
5. Review the trailing 5 calls monthly. Look at *dimension averages*, not composites — the composite hides which muscle is weak.

---

## Part A — Pre-Call Gate (scored before the meeting)

Four criteria. Each is **Yes / Partial / No**. Score Yes = 1, Partial = 0.5, No = 0.

| # | Criterion | What counts as Yes |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Trigger event** | A funded project, modernization program, obsolescence deadline, new line, expansion, or capital cycle with a date attached. "They're in our territory" is not a trigger. |
| A2 | **Named pain in the buyer's words** | Something they said — on a call, in an email, in a public filing, on LinkedIn — that costs them time or money. Not a pain you inferred from your architecture. |
| A3 | **Repeatability** | Multiple sites, multiple lines, a recurring rebuild cadence, or a standardization mandate. EAE's economics need reuse to pencil. A single one-off machine rarely does. |
| A4 | **Budget proximity** | Someone with budget authority is in the room, or one warm introduction away, and you know their name. |

### Gate thresholds

| Score | Meeting type authorized |
|---|---|
| **3.0 – 4.0** | Full first meeting. Engineer may attend. Bring the demo environment. |
| **2.0 – 2.5** | 30-minute discovery call, you only. No engineer, no deck, no partner. |
| **0 – 1.5** | 20-minute qualification call, or decline. **Do not spend engineer time.** |

> **The gate exists to let you say no.** Booking a meeting is not progress. A calendar full of 1.5-gate meetings is what "swings, but not great swings" looks like from the inside, and it is also what makes a seller lean — when every meeting has to work, buyers can feel it. Protecting the gate is what buys the posture.

### Gate exception — Integrator / Channel track

If the counterparty is a **systems integrator, OEM, or machine builder** rather than an end user, A1–A4 do not apply. Score the Integrator Gate instead:

| # | Criterion | What counts as Yes |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Scaling motive** | They want to grow their book, take on more jobs, or serve segments they currently can't. |
| I2 | **Segment fit** | They serve manufacturing/machine-level work where EAE's application-portability story creates leverage for *them*. |
| I3 | **Capability gap** | Something today limits their scale — engineering hours per job, vendor lock, platform breadth, headcount. |
| I4 | **Willingness to learn** | They will invest their own time: take the free trial license, attend the free virtual training, sit with an engineer. |

Integrator gate ≥ 3.0 authorizes engineer time regardless of their own facility's use case. **The integrator's plant is not the opportunity; their customer base is.**

## Part B — In-Call Execution (1–10 each)

Score each dimension. Anchors given at 3 / 6 / 9.

### B1. Opening & framing
- **3** — Dive straight into product or small talk with no structure.
- **6** — Some context set, no explicit agenda or time-box.
- **9** — Agenda stated, time confirmed, and an explicit "what would make this worth your time?" asked and answered.

### B2. Discovery discipline
- **3** — Fewer than 5 open questions; pitching starts in the first 5 minutes.
- **6** — Good questions early, abandoned once product comes up.
- **9** — 15+ minutes of questions before any architecture is mentioned. Follow-up questions chain off answers.

### B3. Question quality
- **3** — Mostly closed, leading, or sourced from feature list ("are you doing any machine learning?").
- **6** — Mix of open and closed; some questions invite a "no" that closes a door.
- **9** — Open, non-leading, buyer-centered. No question can disqualify your own solution when asked a different way.

### B4. Listening & talk ratio
- **3** — You talk more than 60% of the time; monologues over 90 seconds.
- **6** — Roughly balanced; one or two long explanations.
- **9** — Buyer talks 60%+. Longest uninterrupted stretch from you is under 45 seconds.

### B5. Pain development ⚠️ *priority dimension*
- **3** — Pain surfaced and dropped, or never surfaced at all.
- **6** — Pain identified but never quantified.
- **9** — Pain surfaced, quantified in hours or dollars, and confirmed back to the buyer in their own words.
- **Automatic cap at 4** if the buyer named a cost ("down for days to weeks") and you did not ask what it costs them.

### B6. Qualification
- **3** — No budget, authority, timeline, or decision process established.
- **6** — One or two established, informally.
- **9** — You know who decides, what the process is, when the money moves, and what the alternative is if they do nothing.

### B7. Objection handling
- **3** — Deflected, over-promised, or conceded without isolating.
- **6** — Answered honestly, no reframe attempted.
- **9** — Honest, isolated ("if that weren't a factor, is there a fit?"), and reframed against the phased model where legitimate.
- **Never bluff a technical answer.** "I'm not the engineer — I'll bring the person who is" scores full marks on honesty and is the correct BD behavior. Only the *absence* of a follow-through commitment costs points.

### B8. Value articulation
- **3** — Led with architecture (IEC 61499, containers, event-driven) to someone who never asked for it.
- **6** — Value framed, but generic and not tied to anything they said.
- **9** — Value expressed as *their* number: engineering hours per rebuild, cost of a week down, jobs they can't currently bid.
- **Automatic cap at 5** if the Phase 1 entry cost (~$4–6k, no control changes) was never stated. It is the strongest de-risker in the portfolio and it is free to say.

### B9. Next-step quality
- **3** — "I'll follow up" / "let me send some information."
- **6** — Next meeting agreed but undated, or with no defined purpose.
- **9** — Dated, attendees named, agenda stated in the buyer's own words, and a success criterion both sides agreed on.

### B10. Partner & resource orchestration
- **3** — Unbriefed partner or engineer freelances, voices doubt, or contradicts positioning in front of the prospect.
- **6** — Partner present and neutral.
- **9** — Partner briefed beforehand on angle and known gaps; adds credibility at the right moment.
- **N/A** if you were solo — omit from the average, do not score zero.

## Part C — Outcome (recorded after the fact)

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Dated next step with the economic buyer, or a trial license actually installed |
| **B** | Dated next step with a technical evaluator or champion |
| **C** | Next step agreed, undated, or buyer-framed as informational |
| **D** | No next step, relationship intact, re-entry trigger identified |
| **F** | No next step, no trigger, no reason to return |

---

## Composite

```
Pre-Call Gate    : X.X / 4.0
Execution average: X.X / 10   (mean of B1–B10, excluding N/A)
Outcome          : A–F
```

Track all three separately. A high execution score behind a low gate score means you are getting good at meetings that should not have happened.

## Named anti-patterns

Recurring failure modes. Flag by name in the call log so they become countable.

| Name | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pitch-on-No** | Buyer says the pain isn't a pain; you respond with product instead of another question. |
| **Self-Disqualifying Question** | You narrow a real pain down to the one component where you lose ("how often is it the PLC itself?"). |
| **Architecture Reflex** | Reaching for IEC 61499 / containers / event-driven when the buyer asked a business question. |
| **Buried Signal** | Buyer names a multi-year cadence or program in the first two minutes and you never return to it. |
| **Unbriefed Partner** | Channel or internal resource voices doubt about the product in front of the prospect. |
| **Price Silence** | Ending a meeting without stating the ~$4–6k Phase 1 entry. |
| **Deference Tell** | "I don't want to take up your time" / apologizing for the meeting / closing on a request for feedback about yourself. Reads as low-status and invites the buyer to discount the meeting. |
| **Empty-Handed Demo** | Buyer wants to see the environment; you don't have it open. |
